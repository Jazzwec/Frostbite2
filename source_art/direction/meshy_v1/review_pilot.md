1. **Design the reference for the gameplay camera.**  
   **See:** Image 2 presents a large, readable façade. In Image 1, the elevated camera makes the snow roof dominate and compresses the sign and entrance. Image 3 keeps building identities readable from above.  
   **Phone impact:** Players see “snow-covered hut” before “storehouse.”  
   **Change:** Generate references at the actual game camera angle and orthographic projection. Ask for a broad front gable, deep building body and oversized functional features. Judge every candidate at its intended **150–200 px screen width**, before spending on 3D generation.

2. **Reduce the snow overhang and recover the storehouse’s identity.**  
   **See:** The pilot’s drooping front snow edge nearly swallows the sign; the reference’s ochre gable and sun symbol read much more clearly.  
   **Phone impact:** Its strongest identifying features disappear, making buildings harder to distinguish and remember.  
   **Change:** Reduce the **front snow overhang by roughly 20–25%**, exposing more ochre above the door. Enlarge the sign about **30%** and simplify its symbol to a thick ring with four short rays. Keep the sign clear of both snow and door trim from the gameplay view.

3. **Flatten small texture noise, not the entire painting.**  
   **See:** The pilot has pale, weakly separated surfaces, while some door and crate details collapse into dark marks. Image 3 uses broad washes with clear structural accents.  
   **Phone impact:** Fine detail becomes visual dirt while major forms lose definition.  
   **Change:** Preserve three deliberate value groups: **light snow, mid-value ochre walls, dark warm-brown timber**. Remove baked directional highlights, tiny scratches and deep painted crevice shadows; retain broad pigment variation. Use warm ivory snow tops and muted blue-grey shadow areas. If flattening is global, replace it with separate snow, wood, stone and metal adjustments.

4. **Make outlines consistent across generated and kit assets.**  
   **See:** The storehouse roof has a conspicuous uneven dark contour; the barracks has cleaner, more mechanical edges. The target has selective ink accents throughout.  
   **Phone impact:** Different edge treatments make assets look imported from different games.  
   **Change:** Target approximately **one screen pixel for major silhouettes** at normal zoom, with thinner or absent internal lines. Use dark blue-brown rather than black. Avoid stacking painted black borders with a rendered outline. Bring the barracks and nearby buildings onto the same treatment during this pilot.

5. **Separate chunky construction from miniature decoration.**  
   **See:** The kit reads as broad geometric panels; the storehouse mixes soft snow with tiny hardware, narrow trim and detailed crates. The target simplifies details while keeping substantial beams and foundations.  
   **Phone impact:** Detail density varies sharply between neighbours, and small hardware contributes noise instead of character.  
   **Change:** Specify **corner posts around 8–10% of façade width**, a few large foundation stones and broad door leaves. Omit individual nails and fine plank grain. Combine handles into a readable dark pair. Anything consistently below **two screen pixels** should be enlarged, merged or removed.

6. **Give the building and its props convincing ground contact.**  
   **See:** The pilot’s entrance and crates meet the snow ambiguously; broad rounded shadows elsewhere also feel detached. Image 3 anchors structures with steps, foundations and concentrated contact shadows.  
   **Phone impact:** Buildings feel placed on a board, weakening both depth and the readability of walkable space.  
   **Change:** Sink the lowest foundation stones slightly into the snow, expose one broad doorstep and concentrate shadow directly beneath the base. Keep crates beside the entrance with a clear **one-character-width approach**. Avoid dark shadow halos around the whole footprint.

7. **Validate one small mixed-asset set before scaling production.**  
   **See:** The new bottom-right boulder reads as a mottled brown lump with a white patch; the target uses larger, clearer rock faces and substantial snow caps. The building recipe has not yet proved itself on props.  
   **Phone impact:** Repeated noisy props would compete with characters, resources and interaction targets.  
   **Change:** Test the revised storehouse alongside **one revised kit house, one crate stack and one boulder**. Give the boulder three to five broad faces and one connected snow cap covering roughly its upper half. Compare all four in a native-size phone screenshot before approving the remaining buildings.

Verdict: fix first — the Meshy route is closer to the target, but camera-aware proportions, texture hierarchy and consistent edges need resolving before batch production.
