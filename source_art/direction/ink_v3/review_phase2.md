The single most important fix is **the roof: it reads as a huge, flat, blue-white panel instead of thick, soft snow sitting on a wooden house.** It dominates each building and makes the houses feel like clean geometric models dropped onto painted terrain.

1. **Give the snow roof thickness and a softer silhouette.**  
   Images 1–2 show straight roof edges, pointed corners and a sharp central seam. Image 3 has rounded snow caps, irregular overhangs and visible dark timber beneath. On a phone, the current roof becomes a blank polygon that overwhelms the house. Add a chunky snow lip roughly **5–8% of the façade height**, round the corners, vary the edge with **3–5 broad bulges**, and expose a narrow timber fascia beneath. Remove the bright ridge stripe.

2. **Make the roof lighting describe two distinct slopes.**  
   In Image 2, both roof planes are nearly the same pale blue; the outline does most of the work. This weakens depth at small size. Keep the lit slope warm ivory and make the shaded slope a muted blue-grey, **10–15% darker**. Add a restrained blue shadow beneath the eaves. Use broad colour washes; avoid adding fine texture to compensate.

3. **Break the identical house silhouettes.**  
   The storehouse and barracks in Image 2 share almost the same roof, proportions and front arrangement. Their text labels carry their identity, which costs screen space and reading effort. Give the storehouse a **25–30% wider façade and broad double doors**; give the barracks a taller, narrower body and a distinct window grouping. Keep the shared construction language, but make each function recognizable with labels hidden.

4. **Anchor the buildings to the snow.**  
   Image 1 exposes conspicuous blue circular patches below houses; Image 2 shows pale oval drifts and rectangular steps that make the buildings appear placed on separate pads. These shapes compete with the house silhouette and weaken the sense of shelter. Replace visible circles with **tight, irregular contact shadows**, darkest at the foundation. Nestle drifts against the bottom **10–15% of walls**, with an opening at the door, rather than spreading them evenly around the footprint.

5. **Strengthen the warm, inhabited façade.**  
   The windows are small, pale yellow rectangles; the doors and walls have similar visual weight. Image 3 uses warm window recesses and dark framing to make buildings feel occupied. Increase window area **20–30%**, use amber centres with cream highlights, and deepen the surrounding frames. Keep the glow concentrated at the window, with only a faint warm spill immediately below it.

6. **Replace the uniform outline with selective ink accents.**  
   The close view makes the thin, straight roof and wall outlines especially obvious. They reinforce a diagram-like appearance instead of the target’s hand-drawn construction. Use dark brown-grey accents under eaves, around door recesses and at timber joints; soften exposed snow edges. Make those structural accents readable at the **wide view in Image 1**, rather than relying on tiny plank lines or sign details.

Verdict: fix first — the oversized, planar snow roofs dominate the phone view and prevent the houses from matching the intended picture-book world.
